Built-in policy preset for the Australian Privacy Act 1988 and the 13 Australian Privacy Principles, with the reforms enacted by the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (No. 128, 2024) and the proposed second tranche recorded apart from them (story RG1.3). Exports `preset()` and `rules()`. Depends on `core.policy.schema` and `core.policy.types`.

**Enacted and proposed are kept apart by rule id.** `apa-t1-*` rules are the 2024 Act: Royal Assent 10 December 2024, most of it in force from 11 December 2024, the statutory tort (Schedule 2) from 10 June 2025, and the automated-decision privacy policy duty (Schedule 1 Part 15) from 10 December 2026, which the rule says is not yet in force. There is one rule for each change: APP 11.3 technical and organisational measures, the APP 8 prescribed-countries mechanism, eligible data breach declarations, the new penalty tier and infringement notices, the OAIC's court, inquiry and investigation powers, the Children's Online Privacy Code, the statutory tort, the doxxing offences and the automated-decision duty. Each names the Act and the Schedule or Part. `apa-t2-proposed-*` rules are the Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 exposure draft (31 August 2026, not introduced in the sources read). Each begins "PROPOSED, NOT LAW" and is `log`, so no proposal can block or warn on a request. The other `apa-*` rules are the APPs as they stood before 2024.

**Corrected.** Before RG1.3 was closed, this preset stated that the 2024 Act made inferred and technical data personal information and prohibited re-identification, and attributed the AUD 50 million penalty ceiling to it. None of that is right. The definition change is a tranche 2 proposal, re-identification is prohibited by neither, and the ceiling dates from the 2022 Enforcement and Other Measures Act. The rules now say so, and `docs/specifications/regulatory-defaults.md` section 3 is corrected to match. What is true today is carried by `apa-personal-information-includes-opinion`: s 6(1) already covers "an opinion" about a reasonably identifiable individual, which is why outbound responses need scanning. The 48-hour mapping table is kept as a loke design rule under APP 11 and 11.2 (`apa-mapping-table-reidentification`), not as a statutory duty.

**Unchanged.** Sensitive information is blocked, not anonymised (section 3.4). `piimappingdays` is 2 and `auditlogdays` is 2555 (section 3.5). Government identifiers and credentials are blocked, and `apa-detector-gap-au-identifiers` names the Australian ones loke has no detector for (driver's licence, passport, CRN, superannuation member number: RG1.7a); Medicare, BSB and bank account numbers are detected since RG1.7. `crossborderallowed` is false: APP 8 does not prohibit overseas disclosure, but the assessment is the entity's own and no country is prescribed, so the rules engine derives a deny rule until the entity turns disclosure on. `maxcostusd` is 0.0 because the source figure is AUD and this field is USD.

Sources, read 2026-09-27: the Act as made (legislation.gov.au C2024A00128), the OAIC's statement on the Bill's passage and its Children's Online Privacy Code page, and a law-firm summary of the tranche 2 exposure draft. loke has not read the exposure draft itself. Nothing here makes a deployment compliant.
