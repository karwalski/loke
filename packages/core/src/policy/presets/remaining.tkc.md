Additional built-in policy presets: UK GDPR, Singapore PDPA, Minimal (for dev/testing), EU AI Act (biometric/emotion blocking, 10-year record keeping), and Colorado AI Act (algorithmic discrimination prevention). Each exports a `$policy` constructor and a matching `rules()` list — `ukgdprrules`, `singaporepdparules`, `minimalrules`, `euaiactrules`, `coloradoaiactrules`. Depends on `core.policy.schema` and `core.policy.types`.

The UK GDPR rule list is the substantive fix here. The deleted `core.policy.regulations.ukgdpr()` was a verbatim copy of `gdpr()`: the same five rules, still carrying `gdpr-` id prefixes, one of them asserting that personal data "must not leave EU without adequacy decision" under `jurisdiction: "UK"`. That is wrong on the substance and not merely on the prefix — the UK left the EU transfer regime on 1 January 2021 and runs its own adequacy regulations made by the Secretary of State, the International Data Transfer Agreement and the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses, assessed against the Data Protection and Digital Information Act 2024's "not materially lower" data protection test rather than EU essential equivalence. Source for all twelve rules is `docs/specifications/regulatory-defaults.md` section 6, which also supplies the UK-specific identifiers (NINO, NHS number, UK passport, UK driving licence, sort code) now in `anonymisation` and `blockedentitytypes`, the special-category alignment with the EU preset stated in section 6.4, the ICO's motivated-intruder test, the 72-hour ICO notification in section 6.9 and the UK-US Data Bridge in section 6.6.

`minimal()` imposes no regulatory constraint by design, and `minimalrules()` is still not empty: a preset that loads with zero rules is indistinguishable from the T-24 defect where `@(rules; rule)` silently produces a two-element literal instead of an append, so it carries one rule saying that it asserts no regulatory position and must not be deployed where real personal data is handled. Its `maxcostusd` is 500.0 with `actiononexceed: "warning"`, taken from `docs/specifications/policy-examples/minimal.yaml`, which states a monthly global limit of 500.00 USD and warn-only behaviour. The other four presets have no policy example in the repository, so their budget limits are 0 rather than guessed.
