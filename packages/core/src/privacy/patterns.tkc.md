Regex pattern library for the regex detection layer: one function per pattern, each returning the regex string, written in the subset of syntax toke's engine supports (no \d, \s, \w, (?: or \.). No imports. Twenty-two patterns: email, AU and US phone, credit card, IPv4, AU TFN, AU ABN, US SSN, URL and API key; Medicare, BSB and bank account (RG1.7); Centrelink CRN, passport, driver licence and super member number (RG1.7a); and the UK and Singapore identifiers below (RG1.7a, completed 2026-10-01). Validation is not done here: check digits, structure rules and the digit and letter boundary tests live in core.privacy.regex.

UK and Singapore formats and the specifications they follow:

- `patnhsnumber` (uk_nhs_number): ten digits, 3-3-4. NHS Data Dictionary, attribute NHS NUMBER (datadictionary.nhs.uk/attributes/nhs_number.html): the first nine digits weighted 10 to 2, check digit 11 minus the sum mod 11, 11 written 0, 10 never issued. Gated on the word NHS as well as the check, because about one in eleven bare ten-digit runs (Australian mobiles, US 3-3-4 phone numbers) would pass it.
- `patnino` (uk_nino): two letters, six digits, suffix A-D, optionally spaced in pairs. HMRC National Insurance Manual NIM39110 (gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim39110): D, F, I, Q, U, V never first or second letter, O never second, prefixes BG, GB, KN, NK, NT, TN, ZZ not used. No check digit exists; no label is required.
- `patsortcode` (uk_sort_code): six digits nn-nn-nn after the words "sort code". No check digit is published, so it is gated on its words, as BSB is.
- `patukdrivinglicence` (uk_driving_licence): sixteen characters. DVLA leaflet INS57P, "Information on driving licences" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk, "How to check your driver number"): surname (five characters, padded with 9), date of birth (decade, month with 5 added for a woman, day, year), two initials (9 for none), and three computer check characters "randomly generated", so they are not validated.
- `patnric` (sg_nric): S, T, F, G or M, seven digits and a letter, after NRIC, FIN or NRIC/FIN. The ICA does not publish the check letter's algorithm; the one in circulation was reverse engineered (Ngiam Shih Tung, "Fun with numbers: breaking the NRIC check digit", 2003, which records that the government releases it only to Singapore organisations on application), and published accounts disagree on the 2022 M series. So no checksum is applied and the number is gated on its words, as the Centrelink CRN is.
