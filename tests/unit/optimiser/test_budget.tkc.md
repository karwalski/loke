Tests `core.optimiser.budget` by importing it (X7.2). The file it replaces declared seven stand-ins — `checklimit`, `isoverbudget`, `remaining`, `matchperiod`, `periodlabel`, `createbudget` — of which **not one but `periodlabel` exists in the module**, and that one was asserted to the wrong contract (`"Today"`/`"This week"`/`"This month"` where the module returns `"daily"`/`"weekly"`/`"monthly"`) and passed anyway. Covers: the `$budgetperiod` sum and its predicates (a plain user sum, so `mt` discriminates — T-18 kills only an `X!$err` error arm); the two shipped default limits and their ceilings, read through `microusd` rather than by float comparison because only a bare `f64` **return** is corrupted (T-20), struct fields are intact; `microusd`'s unit being MILLIONTHS, including the sub-cent case that is the whole reason it is not `f64hundredths`; `check` allowing, refusing on tokens, refusing on cost, being exclusive at the ceiling, and **not mutating** (asking whether a request fits must not spend the budget). **The load-bearing assertion is GA1.5 — that `record` accumulates** — made through `check`: two 900-token requests against a 1000-token ceiling, where the second must be refused. `record`'s body was `manager.usage.get(i)==$budgetusage{…}`, a comparison whose bool was discarded (T-26), so every usage row stayed at zero and the budget never refused at any volume. Asserting through the refusal rather than through a totals accessor is deliberate: it is what a real caller observes, and it survives the module's totals helpers being reshaped. Also covers `withusage` (a budget that survives the process), `resetperiod` clearing one period and leaving the other, and `formatusage` showing the recorded tokens and request count that the same no-op left at zero. Depends on `std.test`, `std.str`, `core.optimiser.budgettypes`, `core.optimiser.budget`.
