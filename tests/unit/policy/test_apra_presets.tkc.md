Asserts that every built-in policy preset loads by name through `core.policy.loader` and that none of them loads with an empty rule list. An empty rule list is indistinguishable from the T-24 defect where `@(rules; rule)` silently produces a two-element literal instead of an append, so a preset that loads as zero rules must be impossible to ship: the count is taken from `loader.loadpreset(name)`, through the `$policyset` and `$regulation` projection the loader actually evaluates, not from the preset module directly.

Also asserts: an unregistered name yields the not-found sentinel and carries no rules, so it is distinguishable from a preset that genuinely constrains nothing; both APRA presets map more than twenty clauses each and name their unimplemented controls (`UNIMPLEMENTED`, `UNVALIDATED`, `NOT DISCHARGEABLE BY LOKE`) rather than omitting them; both carry the monthly cost limit their source document states rather than a placeholder zero; the UK GDPR preset no longer carries the EU rule ids or the "leave EU" adequacy text and does name the IDTA and the ICO; the AU Privacy Act preset carries the statutory tort and doxxing offences of the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 and says plainly that the Children's Online Privacy Code is not implemented; and a loaded preset is enforceable — `loader.evaluatepreset` produces violations and `rulesengine.presetrules` derives compound rules from a name.

Depends on `std.test`, `std.str`, `core.policy.schema`, `core.policy.types`, `core.policy.presets.registry`, `core.policy.loader` and `core.governance.rulesengine`.
